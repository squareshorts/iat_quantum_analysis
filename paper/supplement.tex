\subsection*{Robustness to binning resolution}

To assess sensitivity to temporal discretization, the full profiling pipeline was repeated using 4, 6, and 8 quantile bins per participant. Across all three resolutions, the pooled profile remained concentrated in the low-angle regime. The posterior mode shifted from \(22.75^\circ\) at 4 bins to \(17.25^\circ\) at 6 bins and \(15.50^\circ\) at 8 bins, showing stable regime-level behavior with measurable discretization sensitivity.

\subsection*{Bootstrap stability}

To assess the stability of the pooled estimate, we drew 100 bootstrap subsamples of $N=30{,}000$ participants from the full dataset and reran the profiling procedure for each subsample. The pooled MAP was $17^\circ$ in every replicate, indicating that the empirical low-angle solution is highly stable to resampling at large $N$.

\subsection*{Matched-domain calibration in the empirical low-angle regime}

Calibration was evaluated directly at the empirical low-angle solutions observed in the matched Gender--Science and Sexuality datasets. In both domains, recovery remained strongly biased upward, and interval coverage was poor at the relevant sample sizes. Under the present binned Gaussian profiling model and empirical online-IAT noise structure, low-angle profile peaks define a stable population-level descriptor with limited low-angle generative recovery.

In the Gender--Science corpus, calibration at $\theta_{\mathrm{true}}=17.25^\circ$ yielded mean recovered MAP values of $39.04^\circ$ at $N=10{,}000$ and $39.62^\circ$ at $N=30{,}000$, with 94\% HDI coverage equal to 0\% at both sample sizes. In the matched Sexuality corpus, calibration at $\theta_{\mathrm{true}}=19.12^\circ$ showed the same pattern: mean recovered MAP $41.99^\circ$ at $N=10{,}000$ and $38.94^\circ$ at $N=30{,}000$, again with 0\% coverage.
